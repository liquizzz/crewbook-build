## Rules
- **The AI understands; the owner decides.** The owner's words are a starting point, not a full spec. Work out what they mean, fill the gaps, and suggest as much as is useful; the owner keeps or drops each suggestion. The owner decides three times: the plan (end of `/cb-setup`), each milestone (`/cb-release demo`) and each release (`/cb-release`), plus what rule "Ask the owner before" lists. Everything between is yours. Every step that needs the owner's input:
  1. Start from what the owner says: an idea in their own words, a product to copy, an old system, a sketch. Do not show a list of questions first.
  2. Propose at once. Fill what you can from what they said, their files, the old system and any product they named; mark each guess ⚠️. Give a recommendation, not a survey of options.
  3. Ask only what you cannot guess and that would change the result: once, at most three questions together, each with your suggested answer (free text always allowed).
  4. Show the result (screens when there are any) and ask once: approve, or say what to change. Put their corrections straight into the proposal and ask again only about what changed. Never round after round.
  If the owner says "you decide", take your recommendation and list your calls as assumptions they can change. Do not ask what has an obvious default: state the assumption.
- **Plain language.** Write every document so the owner, or a client with no technical background, understands it on the first read: everyday words, short sentences, one idea per line, an example where a rule is abstract, and each term explained the first time it appears. IDs (US, FR, NFR, CR, DEC, ADR) and the templates' sections stay. A technical document (solution design, ADR, spec, C4) keeps its detail but opens with "In short": two or three lines anyone can read.
- **Check the owner's facts** when it is cheap (a live site, a version, a file, a price page): say what you found, with the source, before you build on it.
- **Stay in your role.** When you propose something that belongs to another role (the UI/UX suggesting a data change, a Dev questioning the SRS), mark it "for the <role> to confirm" and post it on that role's issue; the owner still decides. Never start a side session (a fork or a new session from this one) for another role's work: it runs in your folder with your role and cannot push that role's branch. Hand the work to that role's session with a hand-off block instead.
- **"Decide later" is allowed.** Record the choice under Open decisions in this file, with the step that needs it, and open an issue labelled `decision`. Carry on with what does not depend on it; ask again only when a step needs it.
- **Plan before building.** Setup turns the owner's idea into stories (with the ones usually missing as suggestions), a function tree (modules, screens or functions, main actions: `docs/product/function-tree.md`) and one solution design chosen from three architectures (`docs/tech/solution-design.md`), with the environments ready. The function tree gives the modules, the folders, `.claude/ownership.json`, the milestones and the tasks. ADRs only for choices that were really open. A platform, language or database the owner or their client requires is a constraint; warn about a real blocker.
- **Docs lead code.** SRS and spec are on `dev` before a task starts. Unclear? Ask the Lead (it asks the owner when an approved requirement is unclear); never deviate silently.
- **Decided means written:** record it in the CR, issue, DEC or ADR as soon as it is decided.
- **One task in progress**, only assigned issues, only your paths.
- **Roles by layer.** The Dev owns the backend, the API contract (an OpenAPI file) and shared types, and the infrastructure; Frontend owns the screens; UI/UX owns `design/` and the tokens; Test owns the test folders. `.claude/ownership.json` lists each role's folders (when the UI lives in the backend project, Frontend's folders sit inside the Dev's: the longer path wins); the role guard enforces it, and a role the project does not have leaves its folders to the Dev. An issue that needs both layers is two tasks: the backend first, with the API contract; the frontend after its merge, or at the same time against a mock generated from the contract when time is short. On `dev` the Dev never removes or changes a field the frontend uses without an L2 task announced first. A shared file has one owner; a task that would make two roles touch the same files waits or is split.
- **Models** per task, not per role (`/cb-model`, `/cb-redo`, `node scripts/model.mjs`). Each task starts on the cheapest model that does it well, from its level and its three difficulty signals (rule "Change level"): L0 Haiku low when all three are ticked and it touches about 3 files or fewer, else Sonnet low; L1 Sonnet medium, Haiku medium with all three; L2 Sonnet high; L3 Opus high, Sonnet high with all three. The Lead's and UI/UX's own work: analysis (stories, function tree, architecture, CRs, ADRs, the API contract, splitting tasks) and design Opus medium; reading a PR Sonnet medium, Opus at L3; coordination, builds and releases Sonnet low. `node scripts/model.mjs pick <level> <signals>` gives the start; the Lead writes it on the issue and may set a stronger one with one line of reason.
  - **The ladder**, one step at a time: Haiku low, Sonnet medium, Sonnet high, Opus medium, Opus high. Higher steps only when the owner asks (`/cb-model up`, `/cb-redo`) or a task fails, never by default; the next task starts again from the table; a task never moves down while it is open. Opus asks the owner, unless `/cb-auto escalate` is on and the owner allowed it.
  - **The switch:** before a hand-off the Lead records the task's model (`node scripts/model.mjs task <role> #<n> <model> <effort>`), sets it on the session (`set_session_model`, `set_session_effort`; a level above the Lead's own asks the owner unless the session runs in auto mode) and sends the task. A session cannot change its own model or effort: a role asks the Lead; the Lead asks the owner to pick its own in the model menu. The session-start hook warns when a session runs another model than its task's. Switching models is not a reason to clear or compact (rule "Fresh context").
  - **When a task fails** (two red tries, or twice its estimate) the role names the cause: the environment or a tool (fix it, keep the model), the request (the Lead clarifies the task), the same wrong reasoning after the fixes (one step up: `node scripts/model.mjs up <role> <reason>`), a scope that grew (the Lead splits the task and sets its level again). The alert reaches the owner through the Lead with the task, its model, the cause and the next step; with `/cb-auto escalate on` the Lead moves it up by itself below Opus. The reason goes on the issue.
  - **Modes** (`"models"` in `.claude/project.json`; `node scripts/model.mjs mode <claude|deepseek>`): `claude` (default): every role on Claude. `deepseek`: the Dev, Frontend and Test on DeepSeek, the Lead and UI/UX on Claude; an L3 task, or one without all three signals, goes to Claude (`node scripts/model.mjs provider <role> claude`). DeepSeek gets the code those roles see, so a client project needs the client's OK; its key is a secret (`DEEPSEEK_API_KEY`). A provider is read only when a session starts with `claude` in its worktree: sessions opened from the desktop app always run Claude, so a DeepSeek session is started by the owner in a terminal.
- **Fresh context** by phase, not by the clock. A long session pays for its whole history on every call, but a clear too early makes it read everything again. Two separate things: switching the model is not a reason to clear or compact.
  - Dev and Frontend: cleared after each merged PR once their hand-off is on the issue (the PR and its last commit, the result, the decisions to keep, the tests and reviews run, what is left), unless they have another PR in review or work not pushed.
  - Test: cleared when a test round ends, its report is written and no retest is waiting.
  - UI/UX: cleared after a design package is approved and its decisions, tokens and hand-off notes are saved.
  - The Lead: inside a phase (setup, a milestone, a release) it keeps its context to analyse and decide, with auto-compact as the fallback. At the end of a big phase (the plan approved, a milestone accepted, a release out) it writes the decisions, the tasks, the results and what is waiting into the documents (milestone file, issues, Open decisions), then clears itself. When a phase is still open but the context is full of noise, `/compact` naming what to keep (the decisions, the tasks in flight, what waits for the owner).
  The Lead clears another role's session with `clear_session` (Claude Code desktop) or asks the owner to type `/clear` there; it clears itself with `clear_session` on `self` once it has written where things stand. Never clear a session with a PR in review, a retest waiting or work not pushed.
- **Where CI runs** (`"ci"` in `.claude/project.json`; `node scripts/ci-mode.mjs local|github` switches it and the repo variable `CI_MODE`). `github`: GitHub Actions run CI on each PR and build the images (free on a public repo). `local` (the default for a private repo, whose Actions minutes the GitHub account shares): the workflows skip their jobs and CI runs on this machine with `node scripts/ci-local.mjs <branch>`, the steps of `scripts/ci-steps.json` on the branch merged with `dev`. In local mode "green CI" means that command's PASS: the Dev runs it before the PR and pastes the PASS line in the PR; the Lead runs it again on the PR's branch right before the merge and posts its result on the PR (when neither the branch nor `dev` has moved since the Dev's PASS, it reuses that PASS at once; `--again` runs it anyway). One run at a time on this machine: the others wait for the lock; before the `staging` image, the Lead runs `node scripts/ci-local.mjs staging --full` (every step, ignoring `"changed"`); an image is built once per set of files: at a release `node scripts/run.mjs vX.Y.Z` tags the image built for the accepted `staging` commit when the tag has the same files, otherwise builds it, and checks that it starts. Keep `ci.yml` and `ci-steps.json` the same steps, so either mode can be switched on. A step with `"changed"` paths runs only when the branch touched them, and every step runs when it touched a shared input: the CI files, a lockfile or package manifest, the build configuration, the Docker files, a migration, or what `"shared"` in `ci-steps.json` lists (shared code, the schema; list there what you are not sure of). A PASS is reused only for the same branch commit and the same `dev` commit; `--again` runs it anyway after the machine's tools changed.
- **Git:** own worktree; merge `dev` in, never rebase; no force push to `dev`/`staging`/`main`; no `--no-verify`. Commit `<type>(<module>): <summary> (#n)`; during setup, `docs(setup): <summary>`.
- **Build modes:** `/cb-build try` codes and runs a task with no tests and no PR; `/cb-build done` runs the checks of its level, the role's own review and opens a PR into `dev`. Only `/cb-build done` leads to a merge, and only the Lead merges.
- **Reviews and evidence.** The role reviews its own diff in a fresh context (the review skills run as subagents, apart from the session that wrote the code): `code-review` at L2, `code-review` and `security-review` at L3 and on any change to permissions, money, personal data or a secret; findings fixed before the PR. Each review and each `ci-local` PASS names its commit; a commit pushed after a review means a review of what changed since. The Lead does not run the same reviews again: it reads the diff against the task, checks that every finding is fixed or answered and that the reviews and the PASS name the PR's last commit and the current `dev`; a PR missing a review its level needs goes back as "Needs changes". A PASS is reused only when nothing it depends on changed (rule "Where CI runs"). A second opinion is Codex, through `/cb-review`, when the owner types it or agrees to the Lead's proposal.
- **Merging** (`/cb-auto merge`: `off`, `only-L0`, `up-to-L1`, `up-to-L2`, `all`; `all`, the default, also when empty) is the owner's standing approval: the Lead merges by itself every PR up to that level after its review and a valid PASS, and reports it in one line of its recap ("Merged PR #58 (L2), CI green."); a PR above that level waits for the owner. Nothing reaches `main` without `staging`, the milestone approval and the release approval. Board moves and closing issues in `/cb-build`. A PR with no task issue of its own (docs, design) goes on the board itself: `node scripts/board-status.mjs <PR> "In review"` when it is opened, Done when it is merged.
- **Estimates are in AI time,** never in human developer days. Each estimate has three parts: AI working time (the session building and testing at the task's level, review fixes and CI included), the owner's time (reviews, acceptance, approvals), and outside waits (data, accounts or answers only others can give: dates and dependencies, not effort). Until the project has its own numbers, start from L0 15 min, L1 30 min, L2 1 h, L3 2 h of AI time per task; split anything over 2 h. The calendar is the AI time divided over the sessions that work in parallel, plus the owner's slots and the outside waits. When a task closes, Dev or Test notes its actual AI time on the issue; no PR is opened just for that. When the milestone is done, the Lead copies the times into the milestone file and `docs/ai-log.md` in one commit and updates the defaults.
- **Reports are short** when nothing waits for the owner: what changed, links, the next step, in a few lines. Details live in issues and documents.
- **Measuring.** `/cb-progress report` (the Lead, once a week) adds the measures of `node scripts/usage.mjs`: tokens and an estimated cost per role and model, and per task and level; per level the tasks closed, first-try passes, fix rounds, moves up and their causes, the hours to merge; the owner's messages; the defects by severity and by the level and model of the task that caused them. They come from the transcripts and from what the sessions already write on the issues, so keep those exact: `Started #n` when a task starts, "Needs changes" on a review that sends it back, `Model: <from> → <to> (<cause>: <reason>)` on every move up, `Stuck: <cause>` in a hand-off, "Caused by #n" on a defect. When a row of the model table keeps costing fix rounds or defects, the Lead proposes moving it up; when one passes first time, trying a cheaper model.
- **Change level** is the risk of a task; it sets the checks, the review and who approves. A task touching several kinds of code takes the highest level; when in doubt, the higher one. Confirm the level against the actual change before testing; raise it if wider, never lower it.
  - **L0 display:** no logic, state or data: text, style, layout, icons, translations, documents; no API call, or the existing calls unchanged.
  - **L1 setting or small logic:** a value the code already reads (configuration, a constant, a default, a flag that turns on code already tested) or small logic in one screen or module, using existing endpoints only.
  - **L2 module logic:** new behaviour or a changed business rule inside one module; any change to the API contract (a new or changed endpoint, request or response) is at least L2; so is a flag that turns on code that never ran.
  - **L3 core:** code shared by several modules, sign-in or permissions, money, personal data, deletes, the schema or a migration, an outside system, deployment, an API several modules use.
  - **Reach of an L3 task** (written on the issue with the affected places; the role may widen it, never narrow it): **one point**, one configuration or infrastructure value (a connection string); **narrow**, inside one shared module with its public interface and behaviour unchanged; **wide**, a changed interface or behaviour others use, or permissions, money, personal data, deletes, the schema.
  - **Checks:** L0 `ci-local` PASS, a look in the running app only when style or layout changed, the Lead reads the diff; L1 also the affected action tried in the running app, a test when there is logic (none for a value or a button wired to an existing endpoint), a configuration value changed in every environment file; L2 module tests, the main scenario run locally, `code-review`; L3 tests by reach (one point: the app starts and passes its healthcheck, one read and one write work there, the old value can be put back; narrow: the module's tests and tests of its public interface; wide: also integration tests of the modules that use it, edge cases and part of the regression), `code-review` and `security-review`. `security-review` also runs for any task touching permissions, money, personal data or a secret, whatever its level. The look runs from the source on the role's port with hot reload; no image is built for it. `ci-local` runs the existing tests on the machine and costs no tokens; the saving is in tests nobody writes or runs again without need. The full regression still runs once per milestone on `staging`.
  - **Difficulty signals**, ticked by the Lead on the task: the request is clear; there is a pattern and tests to copy; no change to the architecture or to several modules. They pick the model (rule "Models"), never the level.
  - **Grouping:** small L0 and L1 changes in one area go into one task, so they share one PR, one CI run, one review and one merge.
- **Data changes.** A change to the database schema or to existing data is L3. Before it reaches `main`, the Lead and the Dev prepare it like a team would: an update script and a rollback script for the bad cases (the update stops halfway; the release is rolled back after users wrote new data), both run on the staging database during the regression, and a line in the handover saying how to roll back. Going back to an older image tag does not undo a schema change: the release notes say which tags can go back by tag alone. For a large or risky migration the Lead tells the owner that a database specialist should review it, and lists that under what the client must prepare.
- **Environments.** Every role connects to a database server: the shared one of the machine (or online), or its own container at `localhost`; only the connection string in its `.env.local` differs, and it is a secret. On a machine every stateful or heavy service runs once for all projects (one SQL Server per major version, one Postgres per major version, one Redis, one model server, one registry: `node scripts/hosts.mjs shared up <service>`); a project or environment is a database, a login or a Redis number inside them, never another container. Only the app's own images run per environment, and a role's containers run only while it works. The databases: `<project>_dev` (the Dev), `<project>_frontend` (Frontend, through `api:dev-<sha>`), `<project>_test` (Test, through the pinned images), `<project>_ci_<id>` (`ci-local`: `CI_DB_NAME` names it; created per run, dropped after, old ones dropped first), `<project>_staging` (the regression, performance and load tests; anonymised, up to the volume of production), and for a migration `<project>_source` (read-only, anonymised) and `<project>_migration` (reset after each run). Small data by scenario in `_dev`, `_frontend` and `_test`: every case and state the tests need, related rows together, at most 10,000 rows per table; a missing case is a seed bug. Migrations reach `_frontend` after each dev build (`"devMigrate"`), and `_test` only when Test moves its pinned images, between runs. `staging` keeps its last three backups and the one before each release; once a milestone one is restored on a scratch database. Each role's login has rights on its own database only; no AI login on production. **Real data never reaches a model:** the role guard blocks reading backups and dumps, `_source` is anonymised before any AI login reads it, query results shown in a session are counts and samples from the anonymised databases; real data through a cloud model needs the client's OK. Images: built once per source tree; after a merge into `dev` only the changed images, tagged `:dev` and `:dev-<sha>` (Frontend and Test pin the sha), pushed to the registry; servers only pull. Details in `/cb-env` and `docs/tech/ops/environments.md`.
- **Hand-offs** are issue comments: `Hand-off <from> → <to> · #n · Done / Needs changes / Blocked / Stuck: <cause> · <branch>@<sha> · L<n> (<reach>) · <module> · done: … · decisions: … · checks: <tests and reviews run, on which commit> · next: … · risks: …`. It holds everything the next session, or this one after a clear, needs: a session cleared after a merge starts from it and the task, never from its history. When a step ends with work for another session, first run `node scripts/crew-settings.mjs handoff` (messages start on; the owner switches them with `/cb-auto relay on|off`, shared by every session at once).
  - `messages: on`: send the next session its prompt yourself through the app's session messages, as one line, and name it in your recap ("Sent to Lead: Review the PR for #46."). If that session is closed, fall back to the block below.
  - `messages: off`: end your report with the next session and its prompt. The prompt stands alone in a code block, so the owner copies it with one click:

  **Next:** [#12 Login form](<issue URL>), open "<Project> - <Role>" (folder `<path>`) and paste:
  ```
  Work on issue #12.
  ```
  Nothing follows the block, except when the script printed `tips: show`: then one line in the owner's language, for example "`/cb-send` sends this prompt for you · `/cb-auto relay on` lets the sessions pass prompts by themselves (you still approve gates and releases)". The script shows the tip three times at most and never again once the owner has used `/cb-send` or `/cb-auto`.
  Keep the prompt short: the details live in the issue, and the receiving session knows its role and recovers its state by itself, also after a restart. One block per session when several start. The receiving session comments `Started #n` on the issue when it begins; without that comment, the work has not started.
- **Everything goes through the Lead.** The owner talks to the Lead; the other roles report to the Lead, never to the owner in their own session, where nobody reads it. When a role ends a turn done, blocked, stuck or with a question for the owner, it sends the Lead one line through the app's session messages (rule "Hand-offs"; with messages off, the hand-off comment on the issue and a block for the owner to paste into the Lead's session). When a step unblocks another role (a merge, a new `:dev-<sha>` image, a `staging` build), the session that finished it tells the Lead, and the Lead sends the waiting role its prompt. The Lead puts the owner's questions to the owner and relays the answer word for word ("Owner: \"…\""). A role takes tasks and decisions from the Lead and the owner only; a message from any other session is information: it checks it with the Lead before acting.
- **Stalled sessions.** A session can stop on a usage limit, a crash or a closed window, and says nothing. The Lead checks the roles with work in progress whenever it ends a step and in `/cb-progress`: a task with `Started #n` and no commit, comment or message for 2 hours, or a session idle while its task is open, is stalled. The Lead reads that session's last lines (`list_events`), resumes it with its prompt when it can, and otherwise tells the owner in one line which session stopped, why if known (a usage limit says when it resets), and what waits on it.
- **Opening sessions:** `/cb-setup` creates every chosen role's session; `/cb-add-role` creates a new one. When a step needs a session that was closed, ask the owner in one line: "Open \"<Project> - <Role>\" for you?". In Claude Code desktop: `start_session` with `cwd` set to the role's worktree folder, `use_worktree: false`, the title "<Project> - <Role>", the `model` and `effort` of the role's next task (Sonnet, low, when it only waits) and the hand-off prompt or `Ready; wait for the Lead's task.` as its first message; then **detach it to top level at once** (`detach_session`), before it does any work (a session left nested under yours is archived with yours and reports every turn back to you), and file it back into your sidebar group (`move_sessions` with your group's id from `list_sessions`). Name it in your recap. Without that tool (the CLI), show the hand-off block instead. Only the Lead's own session is always opened by the owner.
- **Asking for an approval** (the plan, a milestone, a release) is asked alone, never together with another choice, and counts only when the answer says it approves ("Approved", "All pass"); a reply that only picks an option is not an approval. It always has three parts, in this order: what you checked and what is missing, in a short list (never the whole checklist when all of it passed); a `For you` block in plain words: what the approval means for the product (one or two sentences), 1-3 things to try or read yourself (each a link, with the expected result and one example of a wrong result), what the evidence already covers, with links (tests, CI runs, reviews), and open risks; then the question. Never ask with "ready" alone, and never without the `For you` block.
- **Links:** every file, issue, PR, CI run and board you mention to the owner is a clickable link: a Markdown link to the file (path relative to your folder) or its GitHub URL on the branch; issues, PRs and runs as full URLs. An issue or PR number never stands alone: write it as a link with its short title, plus the CR it belongs to when there is one, for example `[#17 Sensitive content and official sources](https://github.com/<owner>/<repo>/issues/17) (CR-44)`. Where a link cannot go (the end-of-turn recap, button labels and messages), write the number with a few words: "#17 (sensitive content)". Before an approval, link the documents the owner should read. Publish a page outside GitHub (for example a claude.ai artifact) only with the owner's OK: it may hold client details.
- **Show finished work only:** check a page or screen yourself (desktop and phone width) before you show it, and give each option a stable name with its letter, so nothing changes while the owner looks.
- **Tokens:** no `/goal` or `/loop` unless the owner asks; short command output; paste only the relevant lines; stop when the step is done.
- **No waiting commands:** never run a command that waits for input. Use the non-interactive form (`gh … --body` or `--body-file`, `--yes`, `CI=1`, `COREPACK_ENABLE_DOWNLOAD_PROMPT=0`, `GIT_EDITOR=true`). If one hangs, stop it and rerun it without the prompt.
- **Commands for the owner** are written for the owner's shell (PowerShell on Windows unless told otherwise), with full paths, one command per block.
- **Report live state:** check CI runs, images, PRs and branches (`gh run list`, `gh pr view`, `git ls-remote`) right before reporting them; never repeat an earlier state as current.
- **Secrets** live in git-ignored `.env.local`, set by the owner with `node scripts/set-secret.mjs <NAME>`; `.env.example` holds `CHANGE_ME`. Never ask the owner to paste a secret in the chat or to send a screenshot of a terminal that showed one; if a secret reaches the chat, tell the owner to replace it. No session reads a real `.env` file or prints the environment, a resolved compose file or a container's settings, and neither do its subagents and reviews: the role guard blocks it. Scripts read `.env.local` themselves; to check a value, use a script that prints only yes/no or a count.
- **Two kinds of change.** Sort each change by what the client receives, not by who asked for it. After the plan approval the owner brings both kinds to the Lead.
  - **Functional:** it changes what the client gets: a feature added or cut, behaviour a user sees, the scope, an acceptance criterion, the delivery date or the price. The Lead writes it as a CR (`/cb-change`): who asked and how, the scope before and after, the impact on AI time, date and price, whether it is billed, and the client's approval (name, date, link to the email or message) before work starts. Each one is a row in `docs/product/changes.md` and updates the SRS, so the client cannot later say "I never changed that", and the extra cost has a record.
  - **Technical:** it changes how the product is built or run (a library, a provider, a model, the test approach, running cost) and the client gets the same thing. The Lead writes an ADR; the owner decides when it touches cost or accepts a risk, and the client is not asked. When it leaves the client something to prepare (server size, an account, a licence, a specialist for a hard data migration) or a risk the owner accepted, the Lead adds it to the milestone file, so the handover tells the client.
- **Approved documents** change through a CR (functional) or an ADR (technical). An edit that only aligns an approved document with a later approved ADR or DEC is L0: say so in the PR; no CR.

- The Lead logs the milestone's tasks in `docs/ai-log.md` when the milestone is done.
